Privacy Policy — Guess When

Last updated 20 November 2026

Guess When is a history trivia game published by KozzzyGames (package com.kozzzygames.guesswhen). This policy explains what the game stores, what it sends off your device, and who else can see it.

The game itself does not ask for your real name, email address or phone number, and it has no account to sign up for. You are identified only by a randomly generated ID and by a nickname you choose yourself. It does not use GPS or precise location; however, the advertising it shows infers your approximate location from your IP address (see Advertising below).

One thing worth knowing about the nickname. When the game first asks what to call you, it pre-fills the box with your device's name — because that is usually a better guess than "Player". Some devices are named after their owner. Nothing is sent anywhere until you confirm, and you can type anything you like over it, but if you accept the suggestion then that is the name other players will see. You can change it later from your Profile.

Google may additionally process technical information, such as your IP address, device type and the version of its software in the game, in the course of operating these services, and keeps service logs for that purpose. The game does not include any crash-reporting or analytics service. See Firebase's privacy information.

The card data, notices and poll texts are downloaded from our own storage at img.kozzzygames.com, which is run by Cloudflare. Like any web server, it receives your IP address with each request. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Pictures on the cards

The photographs and artwork shown on question cards are not stored by us. They are freely licensed images downloaded directly from Wikimedia Commons (upload.wikimedia.org) when the cards that use them are added to your game, and then kept on your device so they are available offline and are never fetched twice. This means your device makes a request to Wikimedia, and the Wikimedia Foundation may log it in the ordinary course of serving the file. See the Wikimedia Foundation privacy policy. Every image is credited, with its licence, under Settings → Image credits.

Helping to improve the game (optional)

After you have finished three games, the game may ask “Help improve the game?” If you say yes, it sends us records of your games, which we use only to improve the game: to tune the cards, the topics and the difficulty. If you say no, or close the question without answering, nothing is sent. The game works exactly the same either way, and nothing in it rewards you for saying yes.

What is sent. For each match: the game mode and its settings, the “seed” that decides which cards are dealt, where you placed each card, the path and items you chose on the map, the lifelines you used, your score, how far you got, when the match started and how long it took. For each expedition: for every card, whether you placed it correctly, how far off you were and how long you took, plus the app version and language. Nothing you type, no contacts and no location are included.

How it is linked to you. Records are stored under your anonymous user ID, the same one used for your statistics. They are never shown to other players, never used for the leaderboard, and never published or shared with anyone, not even as totals or summaries.

We may collect less than this. We may record only a share of the players who agreed, or pause collection altogether, and records are normally sent only over Wi-Fi. The question and the setting appear only while collection is running.

Changing your mind. Turn off Settings → Help improve the game at any time. That stops all sending, deletes anything not yet sent from your phone, and deletes the records already sent to our servers at once. If you are offline, the deletion happens as soon as the game is next online. If you uninstall the game before that, email us with your player tag and we will delete them.

How long it is kept. Each record is deleted 12 months after it was sent, or sooner if you turn the setting off.

Who processes it. It is stored in Google Firebase (Realtime Database), like the rest of the data on this page.

What other players can see

The following are public to other players of the game:

  • Your nickname and player tag — these are searchable, so anyone can look you up by tag in order to add you as a friend.
  • Your best score and position on the leaderboard.
  • Your level and a few profile statistics, shown when you play against someone.
  • Whether you are online, and when you were last online.
  • During a match, your opponent sees your nickname, tag, level and the years you guessed.

Your anonymous user ID is never shown to other players. Please do not use your real name, or any information you would not want to be public, as your nickname.

Advertising

The game shows optional rewarded video adverts, supplied by Google AdMob. They are never forced on you: an advert only plays if you choose to watch one to regain a used hint or lifeline. There are no banner or interstitial adverts.

Google AdMob may collect and process your device's advertising identifier, your approximate location (derived from your IP address) and related technical data in order to serve and measure adverts. This information is shared with Google as our advertising partner. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

If you are in the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area or Switzerland, the game asks for your consent before any personalised advertising data is used, using Google's User Messaging Platform. You can change or withdraw that consent at any time from Settings → Privacy options inside the game. If you decline, the game continues to work exactly as before, and Google may still show non-personalised adverts when you choose to watch one. This choice is separate from Helping to improve the game: agreeing to one does not mean agreeing to the other.

Children

Guess When is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. If you believe a child has provided information through the game, please contact us and we will remove it.

Keeping and deleting your data

How long we keep it. A matchmaking queue entry is deleted when you stop searching, and finished matches within 24 hours. Records of your games, card reports and issue reports are deleted 12 months after they were sent; records of your games go at once if you turn off Help improve the game. Everything else (your nickname, tag, profile, statistics, leaderboard entry, friends, poll votes and online status) is kept while you play, and all of your data is deleted once you have not played online for 2 years.

Deleting it sooner. Records of your games can be deleted from Settings at any time (see above). To have anything else removed sooner, email the address below and include your player tag so the right records can be found. Uninstalling the game removes everything held on your phone, but not what is held on our servers. That is deleted as described above, or sooner if you ask.

Step-by-step instructions are on our account & data deletion page.

Your rights

Why we are allowed to use it. We use the data needed for online play (your ID, nickname, tag, profile, friends, leaderboard entry, online status, poll votes and multiplayer data) to provide the game you are using. We keep your statistics and process card and issue reports because we have a legitimate interest in fixing problems and treating players fairly, for example making amends after a bug; you can object to this by contacting us. Records of your games and personalised advertising are used only with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings without losing anything in the game.

If you are in the United Kingdom or the European Economic Area, you have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to the processing of your personal data, and to data portability. To exercise any of these, use the contact address below. You also have the right to complain to your national data protection authority.
